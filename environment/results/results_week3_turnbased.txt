SECTION E. TURN-BASED GAME (budget -> style -> layout), room 10x10, one run each, train seed 0

Items an agent may pick are limited to ones that are available, affordable, under the budget agent's allowance,
and that have a legal spot. An illegal pick is swapped for an allowed one (small penalty). No round is ever wasted.
200,000 training steps each, 200 held-out episodes (seeds 50000+), same held-out items as A to D.
NOT comparable with A to D: the game and the return are different (return is summed over 3 steps per round).
Metric meanings here: collision_rate = share of rounds where the spot had to be moved;
invalid_pick_rate = share where the item had to be swapped; veto_rate = share where the allowance was too low and was raised.

Reading it:
 - No wasted rounds for any method (empty_round_rate 0.000), so the blocked-purchase failure from A to D is gone.
 - Three agents beat one agent by about 0.7 to 0.75 points (IPPO and MAPPO 8.0 to 8.1 vs single 7.1 to 7.3).
 - The single agent still needs its item swapped 12% of the time and its spot moved 32% of the time; IPPO/MAPPO need
   it about 1 to 2% and 11%. MAPPO and IPPO are about equal (8.04 vs 8.07, within one-run noise).
 - IPPO and MAPPO score slightly above the hand-coded style rule (8.0 to 8.1 vs 7.93). The hand-coded rule does not
   try to maximize this reward (it takes the first legal spot), so this is not a claim that learning beats good design.
   Style match is still a bit lower than the rule (0.40 to 0.41 vs 0.425); the learned agents score higher by using more floor
   area and keeping walkways clear.
Limits: one training seed per method; differences under about 0.3 are within what a second seed could change.
The turn-based game is much easier than the original (random places 4.9 items), so there is less room between random and good.
